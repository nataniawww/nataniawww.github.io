About

My research examines how the rapid adoption of generative AI is reshaping work, from shifts in skill demand and changes in employment and earnings to the evolving nature of where and how people work. I am especially interested in who benefits, who is left behind, and how policy can support workers through periods of technological change.

I graduated from UC Berkeley in 2024 with a B.A. in Economics (with Honors) and a minor in Data Science. Before Stanford, I was a research assistant for Michael Reich at Berkeley's Institute for Research on Labor and Employment, studying California's $20 fast-food minimum wage. My senior honors thesis examined how the language spoken at home affects immigrants' wages and social mobility.

Outside of research, I am probably knitting or reading (Goodreads). Check out my knits on Off the Clock.
