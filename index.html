About

My research examines how the rapid adoption of generative AI is reshaping work, from shifts in skill demand and changes in employment and earnings to the evolving nature of where and how people work. I'm especially interested in who benefits, who is left behind, and how policy can support workers through periods of technological change.

I graduated with honors from the University of California, Berkeley, with a degree in Economics and a minor in Data Science. Before Stanford, I was a research assistant for Professor Michael Reich at Berkeley's Institute for Research on Labor and Employment (IRLE), studying the effects of minimum wage policy. Earlier, I wrote my senior honors thesis on the social mobility of first-generation immigrants using a Blinder–Oaxaca decomposition, and worked on local policy research at the San Diego County Taxpayers Association.

Outside of research, I'm probably knitting, reading, or watching the Premier League. You can find my book reviews on Goodreads and my knits on the fun page →
